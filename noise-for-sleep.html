Noise for Sleep

The best noise color for sleep, for most people, is brown or pink noise: both concentrate their energy in the low frequencies that feel calming and both cover the sudden sounds that cause awakenings. White noise works too but many find its hiss too bright at night. The right answer is whichever one you stop noticing fastest, and the presets on this page are the quickest way to compare them.

Which noise color is best for sleep

Brown noise is the most popular choice on this site for sleep and the one most people reach for first. Its power falls 6 dB per octave, so it is nearly all bass, and it sounds like distant surf or wind. Pink noise is a close second: it falls 3 dB per octave, sounds like steady rain, and has a little more presence in the range where voices and footsteps live, which makes it a slightly better masker in a noisy building. Grey noise applied to either adds fullness at low volume.

White noise is the classic sound machine color and some people sleep well with it, but a majority who try the alternatives prefer something darker. Blue and violet noise are rarely used for sleep. The comparison page lays the three main colors out side by side, and the color slider on the generator lets you settle anywhere between them if none of the fixed points is quite right.

How steady noise helps sleep

The mechanism most people benefit from is masking. Sleep is disrupted less by the absolute level of a sound than by how much it stands out from the background. In a silent room, a door closing is a large change; against a steady bed of noise it is a small one and the brain is less likely to react. This is why noise helps in cities, shared homes, and hotels, and why it is used in hospitals and nurseries.

The research on whether noise improves sleep quality beyond masking is limited. A 2020 systematic review found the studies small and their results mixed, and a few laboratory experiments that timed pink noise pulses to slow-wave sleep found modest effects that continuous noise may not reproduce. The realistic expectation is fewer awakenings from external sounds, not deeper sleep on its own.

Why the sleep presets fade in and swell

Every preset in the sleep category starts nearly silent and reaches full volume over fifteen to forty-five seconds, so there is no abrupt onset when you press play in the dark. Several also use a very slow pulse, between 0.04 and 0.12 Hz, which is a gentle rise and fall every eight to twenty-five seconds. It is well below the rate of breathing and closer to the rhythm of waves; it keeps the sound from feeling like a static wall without becoming a pattern you would count.

Deep Sleep combines near-pure brown with a slow swell. Heavy Blanket is static brown noise with grey EQ for people who prefer no movement at all. Womb Sound is the deepest and slowest of the set. All of them are built on brown or brown-leaning noise, and all keep the pulse shallow enough that the sound never drops to silence between swells.

Sleep timer, volume and headphones

Open the sleep timer and choose how long the sound should play. At the end it fades out over a period rather than stopping, so the change is unlikely to wake you. Many people set it for the time it takes them to fall asleep, forty-five minutes to two hours, while others leave it running all night, which is fine at low volume. The right volume is the one where you could still hear someone speaking normally in the room.

A small speaker across the room is the most comfortable setup for most people. If you share a bed, headphones designed for sleep or a headband speaker keep the sound to yourself. Standard earbuds are best avoided for whole-night use because of pressure on the ear and the temptation to turn them up.

Using it every night

There is no evidence that sleeping with quiet steady noise harms adults. Some people worry about becoming dependent on it; that happens in the sense that a silent room can feel strange after months of noise, but it is not a health problem and it fades within a few nights if you stop. For infants, pediatric guidance is to keep the source across the room and under about 50 dB.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best noise color for sleep?

Brown or pink for most people. Brown is deeper and covers low rumbles like traffic; pink has a little more hiss and covers voices better. There is no research showing one color is better for sleep quality, so try both at the same volume and keep the one you stop noticing sooner.

Is it bad to sleep with noise on every night?

Not at a low volume. No evidence links quiet steady noise to harm in adults, and many people use it nightly for years. Keep it quiet enough that normal speech is still audible, place speakers away from your head, and be more conservative with infants, for whom guidance suggests staying under about 50 dB.

How loud should sleep noise be?

Quieter than you first set it. The aim is to lift the background enough that outside sounds stand out less, not to overpower them. A good check is that you could hold a conversation at normal volume without raising your voice. Most people find they can turn it down further after the first few nights.

Does the sound loop or repeat?

No. The noise is calculated fresh on every sample by your browser, so there is no file, no loop point and no seam. This matters at night because a loop seam, even a subtle one, is exactly the kind of periodic change the brain learns to notice. An exported WAV will loop in your player, but the generator itself does not.

Can I use this for a baby?

Many parents do. Choose a darker preset such as Heavy Blanket, place the phone or speaker across the room rather than in the crib, and keep it under about 50 dB at the child's ear, roughly the level of a quiet conversation. This is general guidance, not medical advice; check with your pediatrician if unsure.

Why does the sound start so quietly?

Sleep presets have long attack times, between fifteen and forty-five seconds, so the noise rises gradually instead of arriving all at once. If you want it to start immediately, switch to a preset from the ambient category such as Deep Brown, which reaches full volume in about two seconds, or adjust the attack control yourself.
